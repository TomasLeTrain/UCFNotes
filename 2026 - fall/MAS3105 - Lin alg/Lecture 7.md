# 1.8 Intrudction to linear transfomrations
A transformation (or fucntino or mapping) $T$ from $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ to $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is a rule that assings to each vector $\vec{x}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, a vector $T(\vec{x})$ in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$.

![[file-20260915101343060.png]]

**Domain:** The set of $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is called the *domain of $T$*.
**Co-Domain:** The set of $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is called the *co-domain of $T$*.
**Image:** For each $\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$, the vector $T(\vec{x})$ in $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is called the *image of $\vec{x}$* (under $T$).
**Range:** The Subset of $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ that has all the images of $T(\vec{x})$ is called the *range of $T$*.
In other words,
$$
\begin{align}
\text{Range } T = \{ T(\vec{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{m}; \vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \} \subset \mathbb{R}^{m}
\end{align}
$$
