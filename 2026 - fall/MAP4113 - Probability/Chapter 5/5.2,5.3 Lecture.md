## 5.2 - Expectation and Variance
> [!definition] Expectation of Continuous Random Variable
> Given a continuous random variable $X$ the expected value is defined as:
>$$
>\begin{align}
>E[X] = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x) \, dx 
>\end{align}
>$$

### Example 1
*A random variable $X$ has is pdf given by*
$$
\begin{align}
f(x) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
C x^{3}  & 1 \leq x \leq 3 \to \text{for some constant C} \\
0  &  x \not\in [1,3] 
\end{array}\right.
\end{align}
$$
*Find $E[X]$.*
**Solution:**
Must first find $C$:
$$
\begin{align}
\int _{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx  = 1 \\
C\int _{1}^{3} x^{3} \, dx = 1 \\
C \left.\frac{x^{4}}{4} \right|_{1}^{3} = 1 \\
C \left( \frac{3^{4}}{4} - \frac{1}{4} \right) = 1 \\
\boxed{ C = \frac{1}{20} }
\end{align}
$$
Then can find $E[X]$:
$$
\begin{align}
E[X] = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty} x * (C x^{3}) \, dx  \\
= C \int _{1}^{3} x^{4} \, dx  \\
= \left.C \frac{x^{5}}{5}\right|_{1}^{3} \\
= \frac{1}{20} * \frac{3^{5}-1}{5} \\
= \frac{81*3-1}{100}
= \frac{242}{100} = \boxed{ 2.42 }
\end{align}
$$

### Example 2
*The density function of random variable $X$ is given by* 
$$
\begin{align}
f(x) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll} \\
C & -1 \leq x \leq 1 \\
0  & otherwise
\end{array} \right.
\end{align}
$$
*Find $E(e^{X})$*

#### Solution 1
By the proposition defined after this problem, can use an identity similar to the discrete case: 
$$
\begin{align}
E[g(X)] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x)f(x) \, dx  \\
\to
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{x}f(x) \, dx 
\end{align}
$$
Also similar to previous problem, start by finding C:
$$
\begin{align}
\int _{-1}^{1} C \, dx = 1 \\
\left. Cx\right|_{-1}^{1} = 1 \\
C(1 - (-1)) = 1 \\
\boxed{ C = \frac{1}{2} }
\end{align}
$$
Then,
$$
\begin{align} \\
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{x}f(x) \, dx \\
= \frac{1}{2} \int_{-1}^{1} e^{x} \, dx \\
= \frac{1}{2} \left. e^{x} \right|_{-1}^{1}  \\
\boxed{ = \frac{1}{2}\left( e-\frac{1}{e} \right) }
\end{align}
$$
#### Solution 2
Can work out everything out fully instead of using [[5.2,5.3 Lecture#^340546]]. Assuming $C$ was already found, find the CDF, PDF, and consequently the $E[X]$.

Let $Y = e^{X}$. Consequently, the range of $Y$ is $[e^{-1}, e^{1}]$.

CDF of $Y$:
$$
\begin{align}
F_{y}(a) = P(Y \leq a) \to \left\{ \begin{array}{ll}
0 & a < \frac{1}{e} \\
F_{x}(\ln a) & \frac{1}{e}  \leq a \leq e \\
1 &  a > e
\end{array}\right. \\
\end{align}
$$
If its assumed $e^{-1} \leq a \leq e^{1}$, then $F_{y}(a) = F_{x}(\ln(a))$.

From this the PDF can be found:
$$
\begin{align}
f_{y}(y) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll}
0 & y < \frac{1}{e} \\
\frac{d}{dy} F_{y}(y) = \frac{d}{dy} F_{x}(\ln(y)) = \overbrace{ f_{x}(\ln y) }^{ \frac{1}{2} \text{ for } e^{-1} \leq y \leq e^{1} }\frac{d}{dy} \frac{1}{y} = \frac{1}{2y} & \frac{1}{e}  \leq y \leq e \\
0 &  y > e
\end{array}\right. 
\end{align}
$$

Finally, $E[X]$ can be found:
$$
\begin{align}
\int _{-\infty}^{\infty} y f_{y}(y) \, dy = \int _{e^{-1}}^{e} \frac{1}{2y} \, dy = \boxed{ \frac{1}{2} \left( e - \frac{1}{e} \right) }
\end{align}
$$
> [!proposition]
> If $X$ is a continuous random variable with PDF $f(x)$, then for any real valued function $g$,
> $$
> \begin{align}
> E[g(x)] = \int _{-\infty}^{\infty}g(x)f(x) \, dx \\
> \text{For } g(x) \geq 0 
> \end{align}
> $$
^340546

### Corollaries
$$
\begin{align}
E[aX + b] = a E[X] + b \to \text{proof by expanding out integral} \\
 \\
E[g(X)] = g(E[X]) \text{ only if } g(x) \text{ is linear}
\end{align}
$$

And
$$
\begin{align}
\text{Var}[X] = E[(X- E[X])]^{2} = E[X^{2}] - E[X]^{2}
\end{align}
$$

## 5.3 - Uniform random distribution
A random variable is said to be uniformly distributed over the interval $[0,1)$ if its probability density interval is given by
$$
\begin{align}
f(x) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
1 & 0 < x < 1 \\
0  & otherwise
\end{array}\right.
\end{align}
$$

More generally a random variable $X$ is uniform on $(a,b)$ if its PDF is
$$
\begin{align}
f_{x}(x) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
\frac{1}{b-a} & a < x < b \\
0  & o.w.
\end{array}\right.
\end{align}
$$
The expectation is the midpoint: $E[X] = \frac{b+a}{2}$
The variance is a more involved expression $\frac{1}{12} (b-a)^{2}$
