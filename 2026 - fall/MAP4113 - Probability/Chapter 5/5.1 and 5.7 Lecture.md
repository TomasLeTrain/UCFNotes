## 5.1 - Random Continous variable
### Probability Densition Function
An r.v. (random variable) $X$ is continuous if there is a non-negative function $f$, defined for all $\mathbb{R}$, exists such that for any $B \subset \mathbb{R}$:
$$
\begin{align}
P(\{ w \in \mathbb{R} : X(w) \in B \}) = P(x \in B) = \int_{B}  f(x) \, dx 
\end{align}
$$
Basically, finding the probability of $B$ happening is the summation of all $f(x)$ given $x \in B$ (sort of addition principle since all $x$ are mutually exclusive and all partition $B$).
This function $f$ is called the **probability density function (PDF)** of the random variable.

Important properties of the CDF:
$$
\begin{align}
f_{x}(x) \geq 0 \to \text{ CDF is always positive} \\ \\

\int _{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{x}(x) \, dx = 1 \to \text{ all probabilities add up to 1} \\ \\

P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int _{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = P(a < X < b) = P(a \leq X < b) =  P(a < X \leq b) \\
\to \text{since its continuous, inclusive/exclusive ranges don't matter!}  \\ \\

\int _{a}^{a}f(x) \, dx  = 0 \\ \\
\end{align}
$$

### Cumulative distrubtion function
> [!definition] Cumulative Distribution Function
> Given a function $f$ as the probability distribution function of a random variable $X$, the **Cumulative Distribution Function** is defined as:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F_{X}(a) = P(X \leq a) = P(X \in \left[-\infty,a\right)) = \int _{-\infty}^{a} f(x) \, dx 
> \end{align}
> $$
> Another common way to notate is:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F_{X}(x) = \int _{-\infty}^{x} f_{x}(t) \, dt 
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!corollary] CDF and PDF relation
> Differentiating the CDF definition gives:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F_{x}'(x) = \frac{d}{dx} \int _{-\infty}^{x} f_{x}(t) \, dt  = f_{x}(x) \to \text{ by Fundamental Theorem of Calculus} \\
> \end{align}
> $$
> So:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \boxed{ F_{x}'(x) = f_{x}(x) }
> \end{align}
> $$
> In words, the derivative of the CDF is the PDF.

## Example 1
*The PDF of the lifetime in weeks of a car battery is given by* 
$$
\begin{align}
f(x) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
0 & x \leq 200 \\
\frac{C}{x^{3}}  & x > 200, \text{ for some constant}
\end{array}\right.
\end{align}
$$
*What is the probability that exactly 4 out of 6 such batteries need to be replaced within 300 weeks? (assume independence)*

**Solution:**
Let the discrete random variable $Y$ denote the number of batteries that needed to get replaced.
Can model $Y$ as a binomial distribution, where about a battery getting replaced is a success, and a failure if it doesn't get replaced.
If $p$ denotes the probability a battery needs to be replaced, the final answer becomes:
$$
\begin{align}
P(x = 4) =  \binom{6}{4} p^{4}(1-p)^{2}
\end{align}
$$
Let $X$ be the amount of time it takes a battery to get replaced, in weeks.

Finding $p$ means finding the probability a battery needs to get replaced within 300 weeks:
$$
\begin{align}
P(0 \leq X \leq 300) = \int_{0}^{300} f(x) dx \\
= \int _{200}^{300} \frac{C}{x^{3}} \, dx  \\
= \left.-\frac{C}{2 * x^{2}} \right|_{200}^{300} \\
= \frac{C}{2 * 200^{2}}-\frac{C}{2 * 300^{2}} \\
p = C\left( \frac{1}{2 * 200^{2}} - \frac{1}{2 * 300^{2}} \right)
\end{align}
$$

## Example 2
$X$ is a continuous r.v. with PDF $f_{x}$ and CDF $F_{x}$. The r.v. $Y = X^{2}$. Find the PDF and CDF of $Y$ in terms of $f_{x}$ and $F_{x}$.
**Solution:**

$$
\begin{align}
F_{y}(a) = P(Y \leq a) = P(X^{2} \leq a) \\
\to P(|X| \leq \sqrt{ a }), \to P(X \leq  \sqrt{ a }) \text{ through } P(X \geq - \sqrt{ a }) \\
\end{align}
$$
Thus,
$$
\begin{align}
F_{y}(a) = P(-\sqrt{ a } \leq X \leq \sqrt{ a })\to \\
P(X \leq \sqrt{ a }) - P(X < \sqrt{ a }) \\
= F_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) - F_{x}(-\sqrt{ a })
\end{align}
$$
So
$$
\begin{align}
F_{y}(a) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
0 & a < 0 \\
F_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) - F_{x}(-\sqrt{ a }) & a \geq 0
\end{array} \right. \\
\end{align}
$$

Now for the PDF, can simply differentiate the CDF to get the PDF.
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{da} F_{y}(a) = \frac{d}{da}\left( F_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) - F_{x}(-\sqrt{ a }) \right)  \\
= f_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) \frac{d}{da}\sqrt{ a } - f_{x}(-\sqrt{ a }) \frac{d}{da}\sqrt{ a } \\
= f_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) \frac{1}{2\sqrt{ a }} - f_{x}(-\sqrt{ a }) \frac{1}{2\sqrt{ a }}\\ \\
= \frac{f_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) - f_{x}(-\sqrt{ a })}{2\sqrt{ a }} 
\end{align}
$$
So,
$$
\begin{align}
\boxed{ f_{y}(a) = \left\{\begin{array}{ll}
\frac{f_{x}(\sqrt{ a }) - f_{x}(-\sqrt{ a })}{2\sqrt{ a }} & a \geq 0 \\
0 & a < 0
\end{array}\right. }
\end{align}
$$
